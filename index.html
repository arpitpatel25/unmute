// the actual bottleneck

Your agents aren’t the slow part. Starting them is.

Claude Code and Codex can already do an enormous amount of work. What they can’t do is start themselves. So the ceiling isn’t the model — it’s how many threads of work one person can open, steer and keep track of before it stops being worth the trouble.

That number is small, and it’s small for boring reasons: find the window, start the session, name it, prompt it, wait, switch, lose your place. unmute deletes that entire step. You say the thing; it’s dispatched.

// what changes

  • Six terminals, none of them labelled. → one surface that tells you which session is waiting on you, and stays quiet about the rest.
  • An idea you don’t bother acting on. → say it in passing; it’s already running.
  • One task at a time, because that’s all you can hold. → five in flight, and you’re not the one holding them.
  • Hands on the keyboard to start anything. → dispatch with your hands full, or walking away from the desk.

// the dispatch key

Dictation types it. Remote does it.

Hold right ⌥, say the thing, it happens — on your actual Mac. The ones that touch apps and browsers run in the background, so nothing snatches your screen mid-thought.

  • “what’s eating my battery — and kill it.”
  • “rename these 40 screenshots by date.”
  • “open Skyscanner, London to Tokyo, next Friday, cheapest.”
  • “total every invoice in my Downloads and put it in a sheet.”
  • “go through my last 50 emails and flag the 3 that actually need me.”

// the key that talks to unmute

Right ⌘ doesn’t talk to a session. It talks to unmute.

  • “what did I say about pricing last week?” — it searches what you actually dictated. Nothing else on your machine can look there.
  • “remember I always want X.” — it keeps a memory, and the memory survives.
  • “what was I working on yesterday?” — read off disk, not off an index built behind your back.
  • “take this and run with it.” — hands the work out as its own task.

// meetings

Double-tap left ⌃. Get notes.

It taps system audio and your mic and writes the thing up. The notes are written by your own connected CLI — never a cloud model. We had one in the fallback path and pulled it before shipping: a third vendor quietly receiving the full text of your meetings isn’t a trade we’ll make on your behalf.

// and yes, it dictates

Under a second to text. Always.

Not the reason to buy it — everyone sells dictation. It just happens to be the fastest one on the Mac, and it is the same key you already have your finger on.

10 seconds of audio or 10 minutes — same sub-1-second latency, every single time. We’re not going to explain dictation to you. We’re going to out-run everyone who ever sold it to you.

And your words don’t die. The instant the network wobbles it falls back on-device, automatically — no toggle, no spinner, no “something went wrong.” Wispr Flow couldn’t keep a sentence alive through a hiccup. So we had to.

  • Raw by default. Most of what you dictate is a prompt. Prompts don’t need commas. No auto-punctuation, no autocorrect editing you into a stranger.
  • Shape it out loud. Select text, hold Caps Lock, say “three bullets,” “less corporate.” Two seconds.
  • The scratchpad. Copy an error, a URL, a screenshot mid-sentence — it’s held and composed into what gets pasted, not eaten by the clipboard.

Ten things running. One question: whose move is it.

Every session is either our move — it’s working, leave it alone — or your move: blocked, failed, or done and waiting on you. That’s the only thing unmute sorts by. Not newest. Not loudest.

It all renders in the notch — the menubar row and the display cutout, which turns out to be exactly the right amount of screen for this. Attention gets pulled, never grabbed. Nothing modal, nothing steals focus. One calm spoken line when something becomes yours, and then it stands down.

// why you can trust it

unmute is dumb. Deliberately, aggressively dumb.

  • We didn’t train a model. The best one already exists, you’re already paying for it, and ours would only get in the way. We gave it ears. That’s the innovation.
  • Remote is your own session. We used to rewrite it — our scaffolding pasted in as if you’d typed it, our model pinned over your choice. Two people said their terminal was full of our furniture and that Claude Code worked worse under us. Both were right. It’s deleted. We start an ordinary session and watch from outside.
  • Your files are yours. Agent work runs on your subscription, never ours. Nothing writes into your skills folder without you saying so.
  • Offline dictation is free forever. No account. Runs on your Mac. Charging for that would be rude.

// "won't Anthropic just ship voice mode?"

Probably. And it will be voice for Claude. unmute is where your context lives — across every harness, every repo, every session, at the level of the operating system rather than inside one app. A harness can only ever add voice to itself.

Claude Code and Codex today. Every harness we can reach, once the interface itself is proven. Doing it in that order is a choice, not a gap.

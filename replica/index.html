Unmute — native UI replica

Every element on this page is real DOM, transcribed from the Swift and TSX sources rather than drawn from a screenshot. Metrics come from PillView.swift, Theme.swift, Waveform.swift and NotetakerWidget.tsx; the status hues are Apple's own system colours, read out of AppKit in dark appearance. Motion durations and curves are the ones the app uses.

1 · The pill, state by state

The nine phases of PillPhase. Two of them are wordless by design: while you are speaking the capsule holds the waveform alone, and on success it shows a tick and nothing else, because the text is already at your cursor.

2 · Recording, across the three kinds

PillKind is a different axis from phase. Only the formatter is tinted — Remote already reads as itself from its glyph and its agent chip, whereas Caps Lock produced a capsule indistinguishable from plain dictation. Hover any capsule: cancel is revealed and the right edge travels, while the waveform stays exactly where it is.

3 · Processing, and its four trailing affordances

The label itself changes with the reason — processingLabel reads draftOffer first, then engineNotice.

4 · The terminal states

Undo belongs to cancelled, not to success. The error hint is suppressed when the message already says the limit was reached.

5 · The agent · model control

One capsule, one hit target, one panel. The connection dot is gone: the mark itself carries the state — full strength when reachable, faded beside a label that says why. The Agent lane has nothing to offer, so it wears its own colour and does not accept a tap at all.

6 · The selector panel

Columns rather than a stacked list, because Codex's three axes are about thirteen rows — taller than the space above a pill already near the bottom of the screen. Which shape it takes is decided by what the engine sent, never by the backend's name. Click a row.

7 · Chips

Square frames, so the capsule is a circle. The whole chip is the button, not the glyph inside it. The scratchpad chip arms and disarms only — it never sends. Both are live.

8 · Narration chips

One at a time, with strict precedence: mic narration first, then noise, then quiet. Two-tier copy — the bold condition, then the dim remedy.

9 · The offline card

Five reasons. Only the recoverable one is tinted and carries a primary button: one tinted thing per surface, and it is the one that fixes the problem.

10 · The meeting notetaker

Bottom-left, 260 × 36, its baseline flush with the dictation pill's. One pill, always: tapping it while recording opens End and Discard in place, and the saved acknowledgement renders in the same pill. Never a second element. Click the recording pill to open the actions.

11 · The same surfaces on a light desktop

Pure black rather than a sampled backdrop is what makes this hold: the capsule reads as a hole punched in the screen, identical on every wallpaper. The rim is drawn inside the shape, so its full width lands on the black and never anti-aliases against what is behind it.

12 · The notch — the ladder

One shape, drawn straight through the camera housing. There is no second view and no pair of wings butted against the hardware: the middle is empty by construction, the path just runs through it, and the housing and the mass are the same black so the join disappears. The top corners flare outward in a concave quarter-circle where the mass meets the menu bar — that inverted curve is what separates a surface belonging to the screen from one pasted on top of it. Drawn here for a 14” MacBook Pro: cutout 200 × 34, so the fillet and the bottom radius both derive to 10.

13 · The notch — hover, and the displays it has to survive

Hover reveals, and that is all it does: it adds one more level of detail and grows the mass to fit. It never opens the panel — the menu bar is somewhere the pointer passes through constantly, and a panel that opens on approach becomes something the user fights. Hover any of these.

14 · The pocket, open

Between the bar and the panel. Not a rung of its own — it borrows the bar's states and changes only what is drawn, which is why it costs nothing: no new window, no floating widget, just the surface that was already on screen. One card, 348 wide, hanging below the housing. The whole card is the button.

15 · The Orchestrator

Clicking a card focuses that task — the voice address — and the Stage takes over. The sidebars are edge-to-edge rather than inset floating panes, and both scrollers carry a hard scroll-edge so content dissolves under the pinned chrome instead of colliding with it. A your-move card carries a whisper of its own status hue mapped into the fill, so the wall sorts itself visually before you read a word. The tabs and the workspace rail are live.

16 · A task, opened

Your message is a right-aligned bubble, never full width. The whole tool run collapses to one line — “Worked for 24s” — with a rule under it; the answer is left-aligned, full width, no label and no avatar. Click a work line to open its steps. The composer's send button is the one tinted thing on the surface.

17 · The attention panel

Exactly one task, sized to itself, and it never balloons into the dashboard — that boundary is what keeps this state distinct from the cockpit. It carries the crank: “1 of N”, Prev, and Next as the one tinted primary.

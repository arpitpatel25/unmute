Our thinking

in the way.

Why we’re building Unmute.

You can think of the next thing before you’ve finished typing the first. An idea for a project. A message you need to send. Something you want an agent to investigate.

The thought is easy. Getting it into the right place takes work.

The best tool is often the one you already have.

You have apps you know, files you understand, and agents you chose. We don’t think you need a new workspace to use them. You need an easier way to reach them.

Unmute starts there. Your voice becomes text where you’re writing. A task goes to your connected agent. A conversation becomes notes you can return to.

Attention is part of the interface.

When you have several things running, a list of recent activity isn’t enough. You need to know what needs you.

That is the question behind the Notch. Working means you can leave it alone. Needs you means there’s a decision to make. Ready means there’s something worth looking at.

The surface stays small until you need more. The rest of your screen stays yours.

Your context should travel with you.

A thought is more than the last sentence you said. It can include a screenshot, a copied error, a meeting decision, or a preference you already explained.

The scratchpad holds a thought as it develops. The Unmute Agent helps you find your way back to earlier work. Both exist to make the next step easier.

Clear choices make better tools.

Local dictation is free. Cloud dictation is a choice. Your connected agent still has its own account, permissions, and provider. Those boundaries should be understandable.

We build on an open-source dictation engine because a useful foundation should remain useful on its own.

Less ceremony. More doing.

We want the distance between having an idea and starting it to feel smaller. Not by asking you to change everything. By making the computer you already use easier to talk to.

That’s Unmute.
